## 0. Corrections to the premises in the original request

Four premises in the commissioning note do not survive checking. They are corrected here because they change the answer materially.

| Premise given | What the sources show | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Incentives law is "Loi 2013/022" | The incentives statute was **Loi n°2013/004 du 18 avril 2013**, amended by **Loi n°2017/015**. There is no Loi 2013/022 on investments. | Citation must be corrected in all client-facing material. |
| "Are the 2013 incentives still in force?" | **No.** They were repealed. See §2. | The live regime is Ordonnance n°2025/002. |
| Mining code is "Loi 2016/008" | Superseded by **Loi n°2023/014 du 19 décembre 2023 portant Code minier**. | All permitting advice must cite 2023/014. |
| Cocoa body is the "Cocoa Board" / "SODECO" | The regulator is **ONCC** (Office National du Cacao et du Café). The state development company is **SODECAO** (Société de Développement du Cacao). There is no body called "SODECO". | Use ONCC for regulation, SODECAO for the state administrative apparatus. |

A fifth, softer correction: the U.S. State Department's 2026 Investment Climate Statement describes a "ten-year phased tax credit system." **The ordinance text does not say that.** The actual structure is installation phase ≤5 years plus exploitation phase ≤5 years (7 years inside a special economic zone). Cite the ordinance, not the State Department, on this point (§2).

## 1. Legal setup of a foreign business

### 1.1 Ownership — open by default

Cameroon permits **100% foreign ownership in most sectors**. There is no sectoral negative list. Only **defence and national security** require prior approval from the Ministry of Defence.
Sources: <https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/cameroon/>; <https://primetimelawoffice.com/company-registration-cameroon-complete-2026-guide>

**Do not confuse legal ownership with practical access.** Informal sectoral constraints persist even where the law is open:
- **Mining** — 10% state equity; SONAMINES holds exclusive purchase/marketing rights for gold and diamonds.
- **Oil & gas** — carried state interest on new licences.
- **Telecoms** — licensing, not free entry.
- **Forestry** — concession-dependent.
Source: <https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/cameroon/>

### 1.2 Two registration routes

| Route | When used | Cost | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CFCE** — *Centre de Formalités de Création d'Entreprises*, one per region, under MINPMEESA | SARL with capital ≤ XAF 1,000,000, or single-shareholder company | **XAF 41,500** registration + **XAF 13,125** legal publication (MINFI) | **72 hours** mandatory |
| **Notary** (direct RCCM) | Larger capital, multiple partners | avg. **< XAF 500,000** | longer |

Sources: <https://minfi.gov.cm/en/business-creation-space-in-the-republic-of-cameroon/>; <https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/cameroon/>

**Fee conflict to flag to clients.** MINFI states legal publication at **XAF 13,125**; Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr states **XAF 13,175** and a total of ~XAF 55,000 via CFCE excluding fiscal stamps. The XAF 50 difference is trivial but the *total* is not — budget ~XAF 55,000 plus fiscal stamps.
Sources: <https://minfi.gov.cm/en/business-creation-space-in-the-republic-of-cameroon/>; <https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2024/october/africa-alert-business-registration-in-cameroon.html>

**Reality check on the 72 hours.** The 72-hour figure is a *statutory* deadline. The State Department reports actual elapsed time of **3–6 months** in practice. Advise clients to quote 72 hours as the legal target and plan on weeks.
Source: <https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/cameroon/>

### 1.3 Legal forms and capital

- **SARL minimum share capital: XAF 100,000** (Loi n°2016/014).
- **Fiscal stamps** are payable in addition to the fees above.
- A foreign investor must file a **declaration of investment with the Ministry of Finance at least 30 days before commencing operations**, and obtain a **UIN/NIU taxpayer number from DGI**.
Sources: <https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/cameroon/>

### 1.4 Quality of the business-entry framework (independent measure)

The World Bank **B-READY 2025** assessment gives Cameroon an overall **Business Entry score of 43.01**, described by the State Department as below several comparators. Cameroon's sub-scores range from **63% in Utility Services to 35% in Dispute Resolution**; the **Regulatory Framework** pillar scored **59 points** (a separate reporting puts it at 58.75).
Sources: <https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/sites/b-ready/documents/pdf/economy/2025/cameroon-2025.pdf>; <https://www.businessincameroon.com/business-environment/0801-15582-cameroon-scores-mid-table-in-world-bank-s-business-ready-2025-report>; <https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/cameroon/>

> **Advisory read:** dispute resolution scoring 35% is the number to raise with clients who are considering litigation-sensitive or contract-heavy exposure. It is the weakest pillar in the assessment.

## 2. Investment incentives and taxation

### 2.1 The controlling instrument

**Ordonnance n°2025/002 du 18 juillet 2025 portant régime des investissements en République du Cameroun** (52 articles). It **repeals Loi n°2013/004 and Loi n°2017/015** (Art. 51). It was **ratified later in 2025 and formally operationalised in March 2026**.

Source (ordinance text, extracted in full): `C:\Users\misha\AppData\Local\Temp\ord2025.pdf`
Corroboration of ratification and March 2026 operationalisation: <https://www.businessincameroon.com/investment/0110-16882-cfaf-2-8bn-kam-plaza-hotel-project-secures-investment-incentives-targets-82-jobs>

> **Note on the State Department's citation.** It refers to both "Ordinance 2025/02" and "Law 2025/015." Whether a separate **Loi n°2025/015** ratifying the ordonnance exists as a distinct instrument is **UNVERIFIED**. The March 2026 operationalisation date is corroborated by Business in Cameroon.

### 2.2 Eligible sectors (Art. 3) — eight, plus explicit exclusions

Eligible: (1) agriculture; (2) heavy, automotive and manufacturing industry; (3) water and energy; (4) education and health; (5) air, rail and maritime transport; (6) tourism; (7) large-distribution infrastructure; (8) digital infrastructure for storage and processing.

**Excluded:** petroleum upstream, mining, gas, and commerce & distribution.
Source: same ordinance, Art. 3; corroborated as "eight priority sectors" at <https://www.businessincameroon.com/investment/0110-16882-cfaf-2-8bn-kam-plaza-hotel-project-secures-investment-incentives-targets-82-jobs>

> **The exclusion of mining and petroleum upstream is the single most commercially important provision in the ordinance for this firm's clients.** A mining investor cannot obtain incentives under the 2025 regime. Energy is eligible *except* gas and upstream petroleum.

### 2.3 Eligibility test — two of five (Art. 7)

An investor must satisfy **at least two** of:

1. **One job per XAF 50,000,000 invested**
2. **At least 50%** of inputs from Cameroonian natural resources
3. **At least 40% Cameroonian goods** in the turnover (large-distribution activities)
4. **At least +30% value added** over the standard
5. **Exports ≥ 25% of turnover** excluding taxes

Source: same ordinance, Art. 7.

### 2.4 The two phases of relief

**Installation phase — Art. 10, maximum 5 years**
Full exemption from customs duty, VAT and registration duty. Patent exemption.

**Exploitation phase — Art. 11, maximum 5 years** (extended to **7 years** in a special economic zone, Art. 14–15)

Tax credit against the corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés), by investment category:

| Category | Investment size | Standard credit | Credit in a Development Priority Zone (Art. 12) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | < XAF 1 billion | 25% | 30% |
| B | XAF 1–5 billion | 50% | 55% |
| C | > XAF 5 billion | 75% | 80% |

Ceiling: the credit may not exceed **50% of declared profit**. Unused credit carries forward **5 years**. Additional benefits: **2× accelerated depreciation**; import duty reduced to **5%**; **full exemption from export duties**.
Source: same ordinance, Art. 11.

**Other reliefs**
- **Extension projects (Art. 13):** 20% minimum increase in production and a 25% credit.
- **PPP co-contractors (Art. 18b):** a **5-percentage-point reduction in corporate income tax rate for the first 5 years**. This is the *only* headline rate reduction in the ordinance — it does **not** apply to ordinary investors.
- **Annual royalty (Art. 48):** 0.1% of the investment, capped at **XAF 5,000,000**, minimum **XAF 100,000**.
- **Fines (Art. 41):** XAF 10m / 15m / 25m by breach category.

### 2.5 Process — the Guichet Unique (Art. 29–30)

A **Guichet Unique** (single window) is created *inside* the investment-promotion body. The dossier is adjudicated within **10 working days**. Existing competent bodies remain operational until the Guichet Unique is live (Art. 50), and grandfathering of pre-existing beneficiaries is preserved (Art. 47).
Source: same ordinance, Arts. 29, 30, 47, 50.

### 2.6 Who the investor actually deals with

The investment-promotion agency is the **API — Agence de Promotion des Investissements**, reachable at <https://www.investincameroon.cm> and referenced by the Presidency at <https://www.prc.cm/fr/investir/api>.

> **UNVERIFIED:** the agency named in the original request, **"ARS-ACE" (ars-ace.com)**, is unreachable — every attempt returned a transport error. I could not confirm it exists, is operating, or has any official role. **Do not cite it.** The IPA (Agence de Promotion des Investissements, established by Décret n°2005-310, operational since 2010) is the functioning body. Note that the Kam Plaza agreement was signed with the **APME** (Agence de Promotion des PME), an IPA/APME distinction worth clarifying per client.

**Track record — the IPA's May 2026 mid-term assessment:**

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Companies approved under the incentive programme, 2014–2024 | **453** |
| Projected investment | **~XAF 8 trillion** |
| Projected jobs over project lifetime | **~180,000** |
| Companies in mid-term assessment subset | **156** |
| Investment *actually made* by that subset | **~XAF 1.9 trillion** |
| Direct jobs actually created by that subset | **>16,000** |

Source: <https://www.businessincameroon.com/investment/0110-16882-cfaf-2-8bn-kam-plaza-hotel-project-secures-investment-incentives-targets-82-jobs>

> **This is the most commercially useful dataset in the brief.** The gap between projected and realised — 1.9 trn of 8 trn, 16,000 of 180,000 jobs — is a realistic conversion rate. The State Department's older framing of "127 projects / ~US$2.3bn committed 2020–2024" is superseded by this.

**Live example of the new regime in operation:** *Kam Plaza Hotel*, Douala (Japoma) — XAF 2.811 billion, 82 direct jobs, tourism sector, agreement signed 25 September 2026 between the APME Director-General and Kam Plaza S.A.S. Group.
Source: same article.

### 2.7 Corporate income tax (unchanged by the ordinance)

| Headline rate | Applies to |
|---|---|
| **33%** (30% CIT + 10% Additional Council Tax / CAM) | Turnover **> XAF 3 billion** |
| **27.5%** | Turnover < XAF 3 billion meeting the stated criteria |
| **3% final tax** | Digital-sector PE from 1 Jan 2026 (XAF 50m digital revenue, or >1,000 users) — or opt for the ordinary 30% regime |

Minimum tax: **2.2%** and **5.5%** (turnover-based).
Source: PwC *Worldwide Tax Summaries* — Cameroon, corporate income tax, last reviewed 14 August 2026: <https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/cameroon/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income>

## 3. Exporting

### 3.1 The single window

**GUCE-GIE** — *Guichet Unique de Commerce Extérieur*, a public-private GIE created in **1999**, is the compulsory single window for foreign trade. Address: **5, Boulevard Leclerc, Bonanjo, Douala**.
Source: <https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/cameroon/>

### 3.2 The e-FORCE Export chain

Customs export processing runs through the e-FORCE Export system in five stages: **prior approval → pre-clearance → handling → clearance → loading**. A **third-generation** version of the system was originally due in 2025; the deadline is now **2027**.
Source: <https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/cameroon/>

### 3.3 Pre-export formalities

The chain begins with **registration in the exporter's file at the Ministry of Trade**, followed by obtaining the **taxpayer card**. Certificates of origin (for preferential access) and the specific export declaration follow.
Sources: Douanes (prior procedures) — **could not be retrieved** (`douanes.cm` returned HTTP 422 on all attempts); <https://cameroontradehub.cm/article/155/en> for the EPA/EUR.1 chain

> **UNVERIFIED — genuine gap:** the official Douanes procedural page could not be loaded. The step-by-step pre-export sequence below is assembled from secondary sources and should be re-checked against the Douanes site before it is given to a client as procedural advice.

### 3.4 Preferential market access — the weakest part of the export proposition

- **AfCFTA uptake is minimal.** In **2023 Africa took only 12.7% of Cameroon's exports** and supplied 9.5% of imports. Only a handful of Cameroonian firms have shipped under the AfCFTA preferential regime: **GIC Afatex** (safou fruit, dried pineapple, ginger → Ghana), **Cameroon Tea Estate** and **Ndawara Tea Estate** (tea → Ghana), **Inoda Industries SARL** (resin), and **Alucam** (aluminium ingots → Algeria). **400 products** from Central Africa already qualify, per the ECCAS Commission.
- **Netherlands, China and India dominate.** Top destinations 2024: Netherlands US$1.04bn (19.2%), China US$900m (16.5%), India US$550m (10.1%), Italy US$350m (6.4%), France US$310m (5.7%). In 2023 the order was Netherlands US$1.15bn (23.2%), France US$615m (12.4%), India US$476m (9.6%), China US$385m (7.8%), Chad US$248m (5.0%).
- Cameroon is building capacity: a **WCO/EU/CNCC rules-of-origin workshop** ran in Douala 29 June–2 July 2026 to build a national pool of AfCFTA RoO experts.
Sources: <https://www.businessincameroon.com/public-management/0107-16399-cameroon-targets-afcfta-rules-of-origin-to-expand-exports-across-africa>; <https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/cameroon-market-overview>

> **UNVERIFIED / premise not supported:** the original request referenced **Nigeria as a regional export destination.** Nigeria does not appear in Cameroon's top-five export destinations in either 2023 or 2024. Nigeria *is* the **third-largest import partner** (US$554m, 21.8% of imports in 2024). Treat Nigeria as a **market for Cameroonian producers**, not as an established export channel for them.

### 3.5 Port of Douala-Bonabéri — the gateway

- **~12.2 million metric tons** of cargo in **2024**; **>70%** of Cameroon's imports move through it.
- **~380,000 TEU** in 2024 — modest container volumes for a gateway of its size; the port is predominantly break-bulk.
- Serves as transit gateway for landlocked **Chad and the Central African Republic**.
- Average cargo per vessel: **11,865 tonnes** in 2024 (Ministry of Transport data).
- **Digital overhaul underway:** the **SIP (Système d'Information Portuaire)** launched **14 November 2024**, is scheduled for completion **14 October 2026**, and replaces the legacy CARGO software. It covers vessel-call management, cargo, invoicing, e-payment of port charges, access control, truck appointment scheduling and booking. Live testing since **3 July 2026**: 53 vessel calls, 26 cargo manifests, 26 invoices processed.
- **AD Ports Group** is investing in a new **bulk terminal** at Douala (announced with its FY2025 results, 17 Feb 2026).
Sources: <https://www.businessincameroon.com/transport/2907-16539-douala-port-advances-digital-overhaul-to-boost-cargo-efficiency-revenue-management>; <https://www.worldcargonews.com/ports-terminals/2026/02/ad-ports-hits-7-7m-teu-in-2025-invests-in-douala-bulk-terminal/>

Cameroon's ports in aggregate handled **23.7 million tonnes in 2025**, up 3%.
Source: <https://africa24tv.com/cameroons-ports-rebounds-to-23-7-million-tonnes-in-2025/>

> **UNVERIFIED:** specific port handling fees, container dwell time in days, and realistic clearance times at Douala. The only sources found were non-citable (Scribd-hosted, undated). **Do not quote these to clients.** Obtain current tariffs directly from PAD and the port community.

## 4. Sector opportunities

### 4.1 Mining — the stated strategic priority, but outside the incentive regime

**Governing law: Loi n°2023/014 du 19 décembre 2023 portant Code minier** (repeals the 2016 code).

## 5. Banking and finance

- **19 licensed banks.** Sector assets approximately **XAF 11.9 trillion**.
- **Afriland First Bank** is the largest by deposits: **18.6% of deposits** and **22.8% of credit** at end-2024.
- **Non-performing loans ~14.3% (2024)** at national level. On the **CEMAC** aggregate, the bad-loan ratio fell **2 percentage points to 15.4%** in the year to **June 2026**.
- **BEAC policy rate 4.75%**, raised 25 basis points on **15 December 2025**.
- **BEAC has halted** a key refinancing facility for productive investment across CEMAC.

## 6. Logistics, infrastructure and connectivity

### 6.1 Rail — a very large pipeline, most of it unfunded

**Central African Republic – Cameroon mining corridor:** **A&S Resources** has secured **US$6 billion** (≈ **XAF 3,386 billion** at the 4 September 2026 rate) from **Exim Bank of India** for a **~1,350 km railway** connecting CAR mineral deposits to **Kribi Port**, to be built by **China Railway Sixth Group**. Design capacity is cited at up to **300,000 tonnes/day**. Commissioning was initially slated for **late/end-2026**, but **port infrastructure at Kribi remains undefined** — the two states do not control the corridor jointly.
Sources: <https://www.businessincameroon.com/public-management/1109-16723-india-exim-bank-offers-6-billion-to-link-car-mines-to-cameroon-s-kribi-port>; <https://www.afri-corridor.com/en/car-cameroon-mining-corridor-the-cfaf-3386-billion-rail-project-could-put-kribi-at-the-center-of-a-new-central-african-export-route/>; <https://dataportuaria.com/en/internacional/logistics/a-s-resources-funds-1-350-km-railway-to-kribi-port-from-cent>

**Douala–N'Djamena:** rehabilitating the **PCDN** (Cameroon–Chad railway) is budgeted at **XAF 11.12 billion for 2026**.

> **UNVERIFIED:** I found **no citable source** for a **Magila** rail project or a Douala–Magila corridor. The original request referenced it. Do not include it in client material without primary confirmation.

### 6.2 Telecommunications — a real constraint on digital trade

- **12.6 million** internet users at end-2025; **41.9% penetration** (DataReportal).
- **CAMTEL** operates nearly **13,000 km of optical fibre** with a combined lit capacity of **1.7 Tb/s**, redundancy points and metropolitan loops, across **four international submarine cables**.
- **CAMTEL uses only ~16% of its submarine cable capacity** — utilisation is 6% on SAIL, 57% on WACS, 29% on the others. Investors flag this as "ROI at risk."
- **MTN Cameroon: 13.4 million subscribers at 30 June 2026.** MTN's mobile share fell from **53.3% (2022) to 43.6% (2024)**. **CAMTEL held 2.08 million** mobile subscribers (2024).
- **Mobile service revenue XAF 631 billion (2024)** per ART.
- Cameroon ranked **last of 93 countries** in the World Broadband Alliance's 2024 **Fiber Development Index**.
Sources: <https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon>; <https://camtel.cm/news/igf-cameroon-2026-camtel-at-the-heart-of-interoperability-and-digital-inclusion>; <https://www.businessincameroon.com/telecom/2908-14936-cameroon-s-camtel-uses-only-16-of-submarine-cable-capacity-roi-at-risk>

> **Commercial angle worth developing:** the ~16% cable utilisation is simultaneously a CAMTEL problem and an available-capacity opportunity for data-processing and storage businesses — one of the eight incentivised sectors under Art. 3.

## 8. What this means commercially — five conclusions

1. **The incentive regime has been rebuilt, and the door is narrower.** Mining and upstream oil/gas are excluded. The eight eligible sectors, with a two-of-five eligibility test and a 10-working-day Guichet Unique, favour manufacturing, agro-processing, energy, transport, tourism and digital infrastructure. Clients with existing mining or oil exposure get **no** 2025-regime relief.
2. **Use the realised-to-projected conversion ratio in client modelling.** The IPA's own mid-term review shows roughly **XAF 1.9tn realised against XAF 8tn projected, and 16,000 jobs against 180,000**. The 2025 regime tightens eligibility further, tightening this ratio again.
3. **Cocoa is a distressed asset, not a growth story.** A 19.9% production fall, a 34.7% export-volume fall, a **63% revenue collapse**, a tripling of closing stocks, and 70.3% concentration in three buyers. Any cocoa-adjacent thesis should be framed around *transformation* — which is the part that grew least, falling 13%.
4. **Mining and energy are the growth story, but they are infrastructure stories.** The US$6bn CAR–Kribi rail corridor and the 1,220 MW generation gap both turn on delivery risk, not on resource endowment. Mbalam's first exports (February 2026) and the US$616m ICC award against Cameroon show both sides of that risk crystallising.
5. **Structure for dispute risk.** A 35/100 dispute-resolution score, a US$616m arbitral award outstanding, ~14% NPLs and post-election unrest in late 2024 are not background colour. They belong in the risk section of every proposal.
